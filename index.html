Seeing and Acting as One: Embodied Perception Based on Fine-Grained Scene Reasoning and Triadic Prompt Engineering under Visual Confusion and Occlusion
Xinxin Zhu, Jialong Xie, Fengyu Zhou, Yue Meng, Chaoqun Wang, Nick Renjn
Shandong University
Abstract
Visual confusion and occlusion are common and challenging situations for robotic manipulation in a household environment. Most existing methods employ coarse object categories (e.g., bottle) or local features (e.g., color) to realize object grounding in target-oriented manipulation. Still, these methods do not consider the importance of text cues on the surface of the target, leading to the misidentifying of similar appearance objects, e.g., vitamins C and D pills can only be identified by the text label of bottles. Meanwhile, they ignore the subjective initiative of the robot in the perception process, resulting in the limitation of perspective in complex environments.To address the above issues, we propose an embodied perception strategy dubbed Seeing and Acting as One (SAO), which integrates fine-grained scene graphs with large language models (LLMs) to enhance the efficiency and comprehensiveness of robotic perception. Concretely, we first design a fine-grained scene understanding (FSU) module to capture visual attributes and fine-grained text of objects in the scene. Subsequently, a target-oriented concept scene graph (TCSG) is constructed, which contains matching scores, fine-grained text descriptions on the surface of candidates, and space relations of fine-grained text and object location. Finally, triadic prompt engineering (TPE) is designed for LLMs to generate control strategies of robot actions such as viewpoints changing, observing, and object rotating, which facilitates the robot to explore more details of the target object in clutter. In real-world experiments, our method achieves a grasping accuracy of 90.1%, demonstrating its effectiveness and superiority.

Overview of our SAO framework

Overall architecture of our proposed SAO. The input image is processed by the fine-grained scene understanding module to generate detailed text and visual representations. User instructions are parsed to extract the target object’s name and its global attributes. These are fed into the multi-source matching module to calculate matching scores for each object in the scene. A target-oriented concept scene graph is constructed based on the above information. Finally, the dictionary variable format of the concept scene graph is incorporated into the triadic prompt engineering module, guiding the LLMs to generate policy codes. These codes guide the robot to actively perceive the desired object from the scene by performing actions such as grasping or observing.
