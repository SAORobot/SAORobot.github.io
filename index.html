Seeing and Acting as One: Embodied Perception Based on Fine-Grained Scene Reasoning and Triadic Prompt Engineering under Visual Confusion and Occlusion
Xinxin Zhu, Jialong Xie, Fengyu Zhou, Yue Meng, Chaoqun Wang, Nick Renjn
Shandong University
Abstract
Service robots usually suffer from visual confusion and occlusion for task-oriented manipulation in the assisted living space or semi-structured industrial environments. Most existing methods overlook the proactive exploration, resulting in limited perspective in complex environments. Moreover, they employ coarse object features, such as name or color, to realize object grounding and do not consider the importance of text cues of the targets, leading to the misidentifying of visually similar objects, for instance, vitamins C and D pills can only be identified by the text label on their bottles. To address these issues, we propose an embodied perception strategy, termed Seeing and Acting as One (SAO), which integrates fine-grained scene graphs and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning with large language models (LLMs) to enhance the efficiency and robustness of robotic perception. Concretely, we first design a fine-grained scene understanding (FSU) module to capture both visual attributes and fine-grained text of candidate objects. Subsequently, a target-oriented concept scene graph (TCSG) is constructed to correlate matching scores, fine-grained label cues, and spatial relations of candidates, which provide a prior for behavior reasoning of robots. Finally, a triadic prompt engineering (TPE) is designed for LLMs to generate control strategies of robot behaviors, such as viewpoints changing, observing, object rotating and object transferring, enabling the robot to actively explore more details of the target object in clutter. In real-world experiments, our method achieves a grasping accuracy of 90.9\%, demonstrating its effectiveness and superiority.

Overview of our SAO framework

Overall architecture of our proposed SAO. The input image is processed by the fine-grained scene understanding module to generate detailed text and visual representations. User instructions are parsed to extract the target object’s name and its global attributes. These are fed into the multi-source matching module to calculate matching scores for each object in the scene. A target-oriented concept scene graph is constructed based on the above information. Finally, the dictionary variable format of the concept scene graph is incorporated into the triadic prompt engineering module, guiding the LLMs to generate policy code. These code guide the robot to actively perceive the desired object from the scene by performing actions such as grasping or observing.
